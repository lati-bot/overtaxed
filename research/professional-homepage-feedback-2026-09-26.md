# Professional Homepage Feedback — September 26, 2026

## Context
Tomi showed `https://www.getovertaxed.com` to several friends. Their immediate reactions:
- The visual theme was well liked.
- The page felt too wordy.
- They could not understand the whole point on first glance.
- They perceived typo/wording issues.
- Question raised: should the site use human imagery?

## Independent review
Reviewed the live homepage on desktop and mobile on September 26, 2026.

### Overall conclusion
The visual system is strong, premium and differentiated. The weakness is first-glance clarity: the homepage is too abstract, cautious and information-dense. It reads more like an audit or methodology memo than a homepage.

### Primary issue: hero positioning
Current headline:
> Less assembly. More room for your judgment.

This is polished but abstract. It does not immediately explain what is being assembled or whether Overtaxed is software, research, consulting or an appeal service.

Recommended direction:

> **Cook County appeal evidence, organized for professional review.**
>
> Overtaxed reconstructs public property records, surfaces comparable candidates and conflicting evidence, and brings everything into one review-ready case file.

Recommended calls to action:
- View a sample case file
- Talk with us

Recommended qualifier:
> Research-stage concept. No filing, legal advice or automated recommendations.

### Information density
Recommended changes:
- Reduce homepage copy by approximately 50–60%.
- Keep a concise three-step story: Reconstruct → Examine → Prepare.
- Show a compact, annotated preview of the case file near the hero.
- Move the full comparison table, exclusions, methodology and detailed source trail to a dedicated sample-case page.
- Consolidate repeated disclaimers. Too many negative statements make the concept sound defensive and obscure the value proposition.
- Use one primary CTA consistently.

### Wording and typo review
No obvious spelling typo was found on the live homepage. Several phrases are unnecessarily technical or awkward and may feel like errors to casual readers:
- “record grain”
- “route for documentary review”
- “comparable candidates”
- “comp-quality”

Use plain language wherever possible.

### Human imagery
Do not use generic stock photography. Smiling attorneys around a conference table would weaken the evidence-led, differentiated aesthetic.

Better visual options:
1. An annotated screenshot of the case file.
2. A before/after visual: scattered county records → one review-ready file.
3. A simple Reconstruct → Examine → Prepare workflow diagram.
4. Later, authentic practitioner headshots paired with real testimonials.

### Priority order
1. Rewrite the hero in plain English.
2. Add a product/case-file visual to the first viewport.
3. Reduce and relocate detailed content.
4. Consolidate disclaimers.
5. Replace jargon and awkward wording.
6. Add authentic human proof only when real testimonials are available.

## Approved design approach
Tomi approved the plan on September 26, 2026 and asked that the audit remain the first step rather than jumping directly into image generation.

### 1. Re-audit the complete experience
Before redesign or asset production, evaluate the live desktop and mobile experience as one product:
- What can a first-time visitor understand within 3–5 seconds?
- Is the audience unmistakably Cook County residential appeal professionals?
- Is Overtaxed clearly understood as a review-first evidence-preparation workflow?
- What is the single primary action?
- Which material belongs on the homepage versus the full sample-case page?
- Where does visual proof outperform prose?

Desired impression: **a precise professional instrument—not an AI toy, law-firm imitation or generic SaaS site.**

### 2. Lock the art direction
- Editorial, evidence-led and quietly premium.
- Preserve the cream, ink and green visual language unless testing demonstrates a stronger system.
- Use real Cook County case data rather than invented decoration.
- Strong typography, generous space and selective technical detail.
- Avoid glowing AI gradients, floating glass dashboards, courthouse clichés and generic professional stock photography.
- Create human warmth through clarity, composition and authentic proof.

### 3. Redesign the information architecture
Working sequence:
1. Direct hero: what Overtaxed does and for whom.
2. One compelling product visual.
3. Reconstruct → Examine → Prepare.
4. Why the work is inspectable: provenance, exclusions and uncertainty.
5. A concise sample-case teaser.
6. One conversation CTA.
7. Move full methodology and detailed evidence to the dedicated case page.

Copy and visual design must be developed together rather than decorating completed copy afterward.

### 4. Explore three visual concepts
**A. Review-ready case file**
A composed preview showing the subject property, official sources, comparable shortlist, supporting and adverse evidence, unresolved questions and professional review state. Leading hero candidate.

**B. Fragmented records → inspectable file**
A restrained transformation from county datasets and record fragments into one organized professional case file. Optimized for immediate comprehension.

**C. Reconstruct → Examine → Prepare**
A refined workflow illustration separating what Overtaxed assembles and flags from what the professional verifies and decides. Likely a supporting section rather than the hero.

### 5. Use GPT Image 2 selectively
Generate 2–3 materially different art-direction variants for each concept that survives the audit, potentially testing:
- Editorial/documentary
- Architectural/systematic
- Warm premium/tactile

Do not use generated imagery for final product text, tables, values, legal records or UI. Those must use real HTML/CSS/SVG and verified case data.

Division of labor:
- GPT Image 2: art direction, spatial composition, textures, record-collage elements and visual metaphors.
- HTML/CSS/SVG: product interface, typography, tables, labels, arrows and real case facts.
- Authentic photography later: actual practitioners with genuine testimonials only.

### 6. Audit every candidate in context
Judge candidates inside the real desktop and mobile page, not as isolated images:
- Does first-glance comprehension improve?
- Does the headline remain dominant?
- Does it feel credible to attorneys and appeal professionals?
- Is the visual communicative rather than merely decorative?
- Does mobile remain calm and legible?
- Does it preserve the distinctive visual theme users already liked?
- Are all claims, records and labels accurate?
- Does it meet accessibility, performance and responsive-layout standards?

Select one direction only after side-by-side in-page comparison. Refine the winner and discard the rest.

### Design hypothesis
The strongest result should resemble an elegant professional case file emerging from verified public records: precise, tactile, inspectable and unmistakably Cook County. The image model supports the direction; it does not define it. Restraint is the standard.

## Decision state
Feedback and approved execution plan saved for the homepage revision. The next step is the full audit and design brief before asset generation or implementation.
